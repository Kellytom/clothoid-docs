# Work items, plates and the board

The project keeps three lists, and they are easy to confuse:

- **P** numbers are **plates**: pictures on the ornament board.
- **W** numbers are **work items**: jobs to be built.
- **D** numbers are **decisions**: choices already made, with the reasons.

## Plates (P)

A plate is one picture on the board, with a short brief and a status such as
*Needs work* or *Done*. There are **85 plates**:

| Range | What they are |
|---|---|
| P01–P20 | the twenty fleur studies |
| P21–P65 | catalogue ornaments |
| P66–P92 | class I caps |
| P93–P101 | class II leaves |
| P102–P105 | presets |

The board is `docs/ornament-board.html`, generated by
`scripts/board/build-board.mjs`; the twenty fleur studies are its subpage. The
board is for reading and sorting, not for editing by hand.

## Work items (W)

There are **26 work items**. The first four are the spine of the project:

| Item | What it delivers | State |
|---|---|---|
| **W01** | A drawing that holds several named lines: traces, layers, a mirror. | done |
| **W02** | The vocabulary of ends: point, arc, round, taper, plus the blunt baseline. | mostly done; ends that turn past 120° are not |
| **W03** | The fleur grammar ported from the old half-stroke grammar. Study 01 is in; the next action is the canonical fleur. | in progress |
| **W04** | The twenty parameter sets, generated from the grammar. | not started |

After W04: the **primals** (earth, water, air, fire, in that order, because fire
needs the turned ends), then the **archetypes** (fleur, acanthus, palmette).

`BACKLOG.md` holds the detail behind each item.

## Decisions (D)

There are **11 decisions**: Scope, Terminals, Lattice, Output, Joins, Mirroring,
Access, Data, CI, Export, Monitoring. Each records the choice and the reason, so
a later reader can see why the project looks the way it does.

One is deliberately open: **D07, Access**. The deployed URL is private and the
shared token is the gate; named accounts are deferred by the operator, and
nothing should implement them until D07 is answered.

## What would make the spine layer part of the tool

The spine layer is central to the curve work, but it is not connected to the app
yet. Four conditions promote it, and all four must hold:

1. The engine can read a spine and build a drawing from its points.
2. One spine is drawn on the 200 × 250 mm sheet, upward, by the normal writer.
3. The clearance check measures it, and one declared touch passes.
4. One fleur study is drawn entirely from these chains.

Until then, "central" describes the intention, not the software. The gate is
repeated in `PLAN` and `ARCHITECTURE` on purpose: it decides the order of work.
